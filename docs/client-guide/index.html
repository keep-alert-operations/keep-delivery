Appchi daily user guide

Appchi brings alerts, ownership and incident investigation into one shared workspace. Use it to find problems that need attention, record who is handling them, connect related alerts and keep your team informed through shared views and notes.

This guide is for people who use Appchi during their working day, with either View or Edit access. It covers daily operations, including shared configuration that editors can change. Ask your administrator for account access, integrations and system administration.

Start here: read Getting started, then follow the Feed and Working alerts chapters. Keep Troubleshooting nearby during your first shift. Use the contents menu to jump between chapters. Use your browser’s Find command to search the whole guide and Print to create a paper or PDF copy.

Your first shift

  1. Check the tenant and your access beside the page title.
  2. Open Feed, or a team preset, and check whether the results are current.
  3. Choose Firing for unassigned work that needs an owner, or Assigned to me for your own. To see every firing alert, assigned or not, select All and run status == "firing".
  4. Open a relevant alert. Read its details, suppression state, history and incident links before taking action.
  5. With Edit access, use Assign → Assign to me when you take responsibility. Add a note describing your investigation and next step.
  6. If related alerts need coordinated work, use an existing incident or create one. Confirm the responsible person and record the handoff.
  7. Resolve only after checking the situation. Closing an incident and resolving its alerts are separate actions.

Getting started

Guide contents · Next: Feed and search

Sign in and choose your workspace

  1. Open the Appchi address supplied by your organization.
  2. If Appchi shows its landing page, choose Sign in with company account. On the sign-in form, enter your company username and password and select Sign in. An existing company sign-in session may take you straight into Appchi.
  3. Check the tenant selector beside the breadcrumb. Choose the team or service workspace you intend to work in.
  4. Check the access label. View access lets you investigate; Edit access also lets you change operational records and shared configuration.

Appchi opens your last accessible tenant, otherwise Global. A tenant is a boundary for data and permissions, not a search filter across the whole company. The same search in two tenants can produce different results. Presets, dashboards, incidents and alert links stay within their tenant.

View full-size illustration

The company sign-in form. Use your organization's supplied account, not the names shown in guide examples.

Global is the shared tenant for legacy alerts; eligible signed-in company users have Edit access there. Other tenants have their own access grants. If an expected tenant is missing, ask the team that manages Appchi access. Being assigned an alert does not grant access to its tenant.

Use your company's normal password reset or account unlock process if you cannot sign in. Appchi does not give you a separate company password. A stale sign-in attempt should be restarted from the page's sign-in control.

Understand the six main concepts

View full-size illustration

A preset selects alerts; a dashboard counts those selections. An incident coordinates work on related alerts. Suppression affects ordinary visibility without deleting an alert.

Concept Meaning in daily work Use it when
Alert One continuing record of a monitored condition. Repeated deliveries update that record. Investigating a specific problem.
Occurrence A firing episode on an alert. A resolved alert that fires again begins another occurrence on the same record. Distinguishing today's recurrence from the earlier problem.
Assignment The person currently responsible for an alert or incident. Taking ownership or handing work to another eligible person.
Incident A shared investigation with its own status, severity, assignment and related alerts. Several alerts belong to one piece of operational work.
Preset A tenant-shared saved query, columns and sort order. Repeating a useful search consistently.
Dashboard A tenant-shared arrangement of counts supplied by saved presets. Watching several operational views at once.

Severity, status and suppression answer different questions. Severity describes how serious the signal is. Status says whether it is firing, assigned while firing, or resolved. Suppression says whether it is normally excluded from feeds, dashboards and automatic correlation. A critical alert can be acknowledged and suppressed at the same time.

Read alert status correctly

Visible status What it means Typical next step
Firing The alert is firing and has no assignee. Investigate and assign responsibility.
Acknowledged The alert is firing and has an assignee. Check the owner and current investigation.
Resolved The alert's lifecycle is resolved, whether or not an assignee remains. Review recovery and history; watch for a new occurrence.

There is no separate Acknowledge action. Assigning a firing alert acknowledges responsibility. Unassigning it makes the visible status Firing again without changing the underlying monitored condition. Neither assigning nor resolving an Appchi record repairs the affected service.

Find your way around

The left navigation offers Feed, Incidents, Presets, Rules, Maint. and Boards. Maint. opens maintenance windows; Boards opens dashboards. Administrative areas may also appear depending on your access, but are not needed for the workflows in this guide.

Open an alert by clicking its row. Checkboxes and row action controls act separately. Breadcrumbs return to the parent area; when you opened an alert from a saved preset, use Back to preset to keep that context. Ordinary links also support opening a new browser tab.

Use the account control at the bottom of the navigation to change Light/Dark appearance or Sign out. Appearance is remembered on the device. Wide tables scroll within their own panel on small screens; a missing offscreen column does not mean the field is empty.

Know which actions you can take

Daily activity View Edit
Read alerts, incidents, shared views and available history Yes Yes
Run an ad hoc Feed search and use temporary filters Yes Yes
Assign, resolve, edit permitted fields or write notes No Yes
Create or change incidents and links No Yes
Save shared presets and dashboards No Yes
Change correlation rules or maintenance windows No Yes

These permissions do not remove other restrictions, such as a concurrent edit or an ineligible assignee. If you only need to inspect data, changing filters is enough. Do not save a shared view simply to customize your screen for one investigation.

Keep your session and work safe

Appchi sessions expire after 30 minutes without accepted activity, or after 24 hours even with continued activity. A visible dashboard keeps the session active, but a hidden background tab does not. Save useful shared changes before leaving; drafts are held in the current tab and are not reliable storage across a reload or session loss.

Changing tenants clears that tenant's current selection and working context. If you have an unsaved definition, review the leave/discard prompt before switching. Access loss can clear protected content immediately; it does not wait for a draft to be saved.

Appchi does not send product notifications in this release. Assignment and notes record coordination inside Appchi; use your team's established communication channel when someone needs to be told about a handoff.

Feed and search

Guide contents · Previous: Getting started · Next: Working with alerts

Use Feed to answer a question now: what needs attention, what happened to an application, or where a particular message appeared. For a view you use every shift, open a shared preset.

View full-size illustration

Feed with synthetic example data. The illustration explains the controls; it does not show your tenant's alerts. This screenshot predates the current quick views and colours: it still shows the removed Active view, and it does not show Assigned to me or Group by.

Understand what one row means

One row represents a logical alert. Repeated deliveries update that alert rather than creating a row for every message. If it resolves and later fires again, it starts another occurrence on the same alert. Consequently, “20 matching alerts” means 20 logical alerts, not 20 deliveries or failures.

An alert's severity expresses urgency: Critical, High or Warning. Status expresses its current working state:

Status What it means for your work
Firing The alert is firing and has no current eligible assignee. Review who should take responsibility.
Acknowledged The alert is firing and assigned to a user. Check the assignee and notes before duplicating work.
Resolved The alert's lifecycle is resolved. Its history and assignment can still be visible.

A Suppressed badge is separate from status. It means maintenance or the source is suppressing the alert; it does not mean the underlying problem is fixed. See maintenance and correlation.

Occasionally severity is Clear when the first message seen was a legacy resolution and the earlier urgency is unknown. Clear is not another urgency level.

Start a focused review

  1. Check the selected tenant, then open Feed.
  2. Keep the CEL expression true for an unrestricted starting query.
  3. Select a quick view. Critical, High and Warning narrow by severity. Firing shows only unassigned firing alerts and Acknowledged shows firing alerts with an assignee. Resolved is useful for checking completed work. Assigned to me shows the alerts currently assigned to you. Press the selected quick view again, or select All, to remove the quick-view restriction; your other filters stay.
  4. Review severity, message, assignee and receipt time. Open an alert's message link to inspect it before acting.

One quick view applies at a time, and each shows its exact count. Quick views combine with the expression, text search and Include suppressed already applied. They do not replace those filters. If you selected Resolved while your expression requires firing, no alerts can match both. There is no quick view for every firing alert: to see assigned and unassigned firing alerts together, select All and run status == "firing".

With Edit access on a wider screen, a row's ⋯ menu offers Assign to me or Unassign, and Resolve or Reopen, without leaving the Feed. The alert's message still opens its detail page, where you can assign it to someone else. See Working with alerts.

Search words or filter specific fields

Use Search text for words in the message, impact or current undeleted notes. Search is case-insensitive, and all query tokens must match across those fields. It is plain text: entering CEL or a regular expression here will not execute it. It does not search old note revisions or every source field.

Use the CEL expression bar for precise field conditions. CEL is a small expression language: each alert either matches the condition or does not. You can start with the examples below without learning the full language.

  1. Select All to avoid an unintended quick-view restriction.
  2. Enter your expression and, optionally, words in Search text.
  3. Select Run. Typing alone does not apply the new query. In the expression editor, Ctrl+Enter also runs it.
  4. Check the result count and any validation message. An invalid expression is an error, not evidence that no alerts exist.
Question CEL expression
Which alerts belong to the payments application? application == "payments"
Which critical or high alerts are still active? status == "firing" && severity in ["critical", "high"]
Which firing alerts have an owner? display_status == "acknowledged"
Which alerts received source updates in the last hour? timestamp >= now() - duration("1h")
Which application names contain this text? application.contains("payments")
Which alerts are suppressed? suppressed == true — also enable Include suppressed

Replace payments with your application's exact value. CEL string comparisons and string functions are case-sensitive. && means both conditions must match; || means either may match. Use parentheses to group alternatives. Quoted values are text; true and false are boolean values.

Select the funnel button, Supported fields and functions, for the available catalog, or press Ctrl+Space in the editor for suggestions. Only supported fields and functions work. Use display_status for current ownership filters; old expressions using acknowledged refer to legacy data and do not mean current assignment.

The last-hour example uses the latest received source update, not the time someone added a note. Relative time is evaluated for the query snapshot; returning to the first page obtains a fresh view.

Include suppressed alerts when investigating missing results

Ordinary Feed results exclude suppressed alerts. Enable Include suppressed to let both suppressed and unsuppressed alerts match your query. To see suppressed alerts only, combine the switch with suppressed == true and select Run.

The switch is necessary even when your CEL explicitly asks for suppressed alerts. All alone does not include them. This setting is temporary and is not stored in a preset; reopening a preset returns to the ordinary exclusion.

Suppressed alerts continue receiving updates and retain history. If a maintenance window has ended, an alert can remain suppressed until its next qualifying source update. Waiting or refreshing alone does not release it.

Sort, page and interpret updates

The default order puts the latest received source updates first. Click a supported column heading to sort ascending, click again for descending, and a third time to restore the normal order. Sorting starts on page one and clears the selection. Some headings, including the derived Status, do not sort.

Use Rows per page for 50, 100 or 200 rows and Next page to continue. Later pages preserve a snapshot so arrivals do not move rows during your review. When New results available appears, use Back to first page. If the snapshot expires, restart from the first page.

Counts describe the current filter and snapshot. Count unavailable, an error, or previous results shown during a failed refresh must not be read as zero. The detail page can show your completed action before search catches up; open it to check the current alert. Rows show times relative to now, such as “2 hours ago”; hover over a time to see the exact time with the browser timezone.

Group results by one field

Use Group by, to the right of the quick views, to show one table per value of a field: for example, one table per application during a wide outage. Choose Severity, Status, Application, Component, Node, Site, Assignee or Incident. No grouping returns to a single table.

  • Each group's heading shows its value, its exact alert count and a summary: status counts for severity groups, severity counts for the others. The grouped column is hidden inside the groups, and rows follow the current sort.
  • Severity groups run Critical, High, Warning, then Clear; Status groups run Firing, Acknowledged, then Resolved. For other fields, the group with the most alerts comes first. Alerts without a value come last, as Unassigned, No incident or No value.
  • A group shows five rows at first; use Show 5 more to continue and Show fewer to return. Ten groups show at first; use Show 10 more groups for the rest. Select a group's heading to collapse or expand it, or use Collapse all and Expand all for the groups shown.
  • Grouping works with the expression, Search text, the quick view and Include suppressed. Each group's own select-all checkbox selects only that group's shown rows.
  • If alerts change after you loaded more rows or groups, New results available appears; use Back to first groups.

More than 1,000 distinct values cannot be grouped: the Feed keeps one table and says there are too many values. Narrow the query, or group by another field. Grouping on the Feed is temporary, like the quick views.

Keep a useful query

Save as preset… starts a shared preset with the CEL currently entered in the bar and the current Group by choice. Review the expression, columns, sorting and grouping in that form before saving. Search text, quick views and Include suppressed are temporary and are not carried into the saved definition. See Presets and dashboards for the complete workflow.

If results still seem wrong, check the tenant, applied expression, text search, quick view and suppression switch in that order. In grouped results, also expand collapsed groups and load the rest of each group. Then consult Troubleshooting and glossary.

Working alerts

Guide contents · Previous: Feed and search · Next: Incidents

Use alert detail to understand the problem, take ownership, document your investigation and record resolution. Readers can inspect details and history. Changing assignment, status or notes requires edit access in the selected tenant; these everyday actions do not require an administrator role.

Review before taking action

  1. Open the alert from Feed or a preset.
  2. Read its message, severity, status, assignee and any Suppressed badge.
  3. Check Details for application, component, node, site, impact and receipt times. First received describes when this logical alert was first received; Last received describes its latest source receipt.
  4. Read Notes and History to understand work already done. If a linked incident appears, open it for the wider investigation.
  5. Use Open runbook when a runbook link is supplied.

View alert opens a read-only JSON view of the currently loaded alert. It is useful when support asks for an exact field value. Source events → View source details instead shows a particular received message. The current alert and an older source message can legitimately differ.

Assign responsibility

Assignment is Appchi's acknowledgement mechanism. There is no separate acknowledgement button.

  1. Select Assign.
  2. Choose Assign to me, or enter at least two characters in Assign alert to a user, select Search, and choose an eligible person.
  3. Check the assignee and resulting status. A firing alert becomes Acknowledged as soon as assignment succeeds; the chosen person does not have to accept it in Appchi.

To hand over, choose another person from the same menu and add a note describing the next action. To release ownership, select Unassign. An unassigned firing alert displays Firing again.

You can assign resolved and suppressed alerts too. A resolved alert continues to display Resolved even when assigned. Assignment does not give someone access to a tenant, and Appchi provides no product notification for the handover; use your team's agreed communication process when needed.

If a person is unavailable in the picker, do not substitute a different owner merely to make the action succeed. Check the intended person and raise the access issue with your support contact.

Add notes that help the next person

  1. In the Notes panel, enter a concise update in Add a note.
  2. Include what you checked, the finding, the action taken and what remains.
  3. Select Add note and confirm it appears.

For example: “Checked payments nodes A and B at 14:20. Only A reports connection timeouts. Restarted its connection pool; waiting for the next source update before resolving.”

Notes are plain text, up to 10,000 characters. They stay with the logical alert through later occurrences. A note is not a new occurrence, a status change or a message sent to another user.

Use Edit note → Save note to correct an entry, or Discard note edit to abandon your draft. Any tenant editor can edit or delete an alert note, including someone else's. Delete note asks for confirmation and hides its current text; retained revisions remain accessible through Show deleted and View note history. Deleting a note does not erase its audit history.

Resolve or reopen deliberately

Choose Resolve alert when your investigation and team procedure justify marking the alert resolved. Confirm the status becomes Resolved. This records a product action; it does not fix the monitored system or stop the sender.

A later firing update from the source can reopen the same alert. If you already know it needs renewed attention, choose Reopen alert on a resolved alert. Either form of reopening begins another occurrence and clears the alert's assignment; assign responsibility again if appropriate.

Resolution preserves the assignee until a new occurrence begins. Ordinary repeated firing updates preserve assignment. Resolving or reopening does not automatically remove every suppression cause or unlink the alert from its incident. When resolving an alert leaves every alert linked to its active incident resolved, the incident resolves too; otherwise review the incident separately.

There is no alert Delete action, and you cannot edit source-owned fields such as message, severity, application or source creation time through alert detail. Use a note for your observations. The current release does not automatically resolve alerts merely because they have been inactive.

Understand occurrences and history

An occurrence is one firing period of a logical alert. For example, occurrence 1 opens, receives ten repeated updates, and resolves; the next firing opens occurrence 2 on that same alert. Repeats do not each become new occurrences. An alert first seen already resolved can show occurrence 0 because Appchi did not observe a firing period.

Detail tab Use it to answer
History Who changed assignment or status, and what changed over time?
Notes What investigation context has been recorded?
Occurrences How often has this alert reopened, and when did each firing period end?
Source events What updates did the monitoring source send?

Choose Order → Newest first for the latest activity or Oldest first to reconstruct a sequence. Source event details distinguish supplied creation time from receipt time. A delayed event can describe an earlier source time while appearing later in processing history. Avoid inferring a new occurrence just because two timestamps differ.

Older history may contain legacy acknowledgement actions. Current ownership is determined by the present assignee, as described above.

Apply the same action to selected alerts

Use bulk actions only after confirming that the selected alerts need the same change.

  1. In Feed or a preset result table, select the row checkboxes. The heading checkbox selects rows on the current page, up to the 100-alert selection limit.
  2. Check the selected count. Even when 200 rows are displayed, one command cannot include more than 100 alerts.
  3. Choose Resolve, or Assign… followed by Assign to me, Unassign, or a person from the picker.
  4. Confirm the result. Use Clear to abandon a selection.

Selection applies to explicit rows, not every result matching the query. Changing pages or filters can clear it. The current bulk toolbar has no Reopen action; reopen alerts individually from their detail page.

Bulk changes succeed together. If one selected alert changed since you selected it, or another check fails, none of the selected alerts changes. Refresh, review and reselect the intended rows before submitting again.

Handle conflicts and uncertain results

An alert can change while you are reading it because another user or the source updated it. A conflict protects those newer changes. Review the latest state and your draft before trying the intended action again.

If Appchi says the outcome is uncertain, the original action might already have succeeded. Use the provided Retry original command control when offered; it repeats that command safely. Do not assume an old Feed row proves the action failed: detail can update before search. See Troubleshooting and glossary for access errors, stale views and information to include in a support report.

Incidents

Guide contents · Previous: Working with alerts · Next: Presets and dashboards

An incident brings related alerts, notes and responsibility into one place. Use one for related symptoms or a coordinated investigation. People or correlation rules can create incidents.

Readers can inspect incidents and history. Creating or changing an incident requires edit access to the selected tenant. Assignment does not grant access.

Understand what an incident changes

Incident information Meaning
Open The incident is active and needs attention.
Investigating Investigation is underway; the incident is still active.
Resolved The incident investigation has been closed.
Assignee The person responsible for the incident. Assignment and status are separate.
Severity The severity chosen at creation. It cannot be changed afterward.
Origin Manual creation or the correlation rule that created it. Origin does not change.

An incident's status and its alerts' statuses are independent. Resolving an incident does not resolve its alerts. When the last firing linked alert resolves, so that every linked alert is resolved, the incident resolves automatically; its Cycles entry reads resolved when all linked alerts resolved. Unlinking or moving alerts away never resolves an incident, and an empty incident stays active. If an alert fires again later, the incident stays resolved. Assigning an incident does not assign its alerts, and selecting Investigating does not assign the incident to you.

View full-size illustration

Concept illustration. Each alert can belong to only one active incident. Historical links to resolved incidents can remain visible.

Find the investigation you need

Open Incidents in the correct tenant. The default Active view shows open and investigating incidents. Use Resolved for completed investigations or All to include both.

Use Search and filter by status, severity, assignee or creation source. Me finds your work; Unassigned finds work without a responsible person. More filters provides identifier and time filters. Filters work together; clear old filters if an incident is missing.

Open an incident to review its description, origin, assignee and linked alerts. The linked-alert section includes resolved and suppressed alerts. Suppressed alerts remain part of the investigation; suppression does not remove their links. An unavailable historical reference means the full alert cannot be opened, not that its current state is known to be resolved or suppressed.

Create and take responsibility

  1. Select New incident.
  2. Enter a clear Title, such as “Checkout failures in the west region.” Add an optional Description covering the impact and what is known.
  3. Explicitly choose Critical, High, Medium or Low. Check this carefully because severity becomes fixed.
  4. Select Create incident. The current form creates an open, unassigned incident; link alerts from its detail page afterward.
  5. Open Assign…, then choose Assign to me or select an eligible colleague. Use Unassign when responsibility should be cleared.
  6. Select Investigating when work begins. Add your findings under Notes.

Titles allow 200 characters and descriptions 10,000. Duplicate titles are allowed; share the incident link for handovers. Edit title and description works even on resolved incidents. Text is plain text; Markdown formatting is not interpreted.

Link the relevant alerts

From an active incident, select Link alerts…. Search by text, select the relevant rows, and choose Link selected alerts. Turn on Include suppressed when a maintenance-related alert belongs in the investigation. The option starts off; linking a suppressed alert does not unsuppress it. You may also link a resolved alert to preserve useful context.

For precise selection, expand Enter known alert IDs. Entered IDs replace the checkbox selection. Use unique IDs from the current tenant, with at most 100 alerts per operation.

An Other incident marker indicates an alert already has an active owner. Ordinary linking cannot take it away. A conflict leaves the whole selection unchanged, so review the conflicting owner before retrying.

Unlink or move an alert

Use Unlink or move… when the investigation changes:

Action When to use it How
Unlink alerts The alert is unrelated and should leave this incident. Choose the operation, select the alerts, then Unlink selected alerts.
Move to another incident Another active investigation should own the alert. Choose the operation, select alerts and a Destination active incident, then Move selected alerts.

Both incidents must be active in the same tenant. Alerts transfer together; a conflict changes none. Unlinking or moving preserves history, alert status, suppression and assignment. Empty incidents remain available.

Resolved incidents have fixed membership until reopened. Unlinking also prevents automatic correlation from immediately adding the same alert back using its old evidence; a later qualifying source transition is required for automatic reuse.

Leave a useful handover

In Notes, write what you observed, what you tried, the result and the next action, then select Add note. For example:

Checkout errors began after the west-region release. Compared with the east region; no matching errors there. Rollback requested through the team's usual channel. Next: check recovery after rollback and review the two linked alerts.

Notes may contain up to 10,000 characters and must include nonblank text. Editors can edit or delete any incident note, including on a resolved incident. Edit note and Save note add a revision. Delete note hides the current text while retaining its history. Readers can inspect note revisions and deletion records.

Use Activity to understand who changed status, responsibility or membership and when. Use Cycles to distinguish the original investigation from later reopenings. Appchi does not send product notifications; use your team's normal communication channel when another person must act.

Resolve, then reopen only if necessary

When the investigation is complete, add a final note and select Resolved. A note is useful but not required. Check the linked alerts separately: they keep their own firing/resolved state. The incident releases its active ownership of those alerts while preserving relationships in history.

To reopen a resolved incident, select Open or Investigating in its status controls. Reopening starts a new lifecycle cycle and preserves an eligible assignee. It can fail if another active incident now owns a linked alert, or if another active incident occupies the same originating correlation-rule group. Review the blocking incident; Appchi does not silently take ownership away from it.

There is no Delete incident action. The current release also has no automatic inactivity resolution or automatic retention sweep: an incident stays active until someone resolves it or all its linked alerts resolve, and a resolved incident is not removed automatically. Explicitly resolve finished work. An older Activity or Cycles entry may still record an automatic inactivity resolution; it describes a past event, not a current timer.

If someone changes an incident while you are editing it, review the new state before retrying. In a membership dialog, Clear selection and reload revision resets your selection to the refreshed incident version. If the outcome is uncertain after a connection failure, use Retry original command so the original action is checked safely rather than starting a duplicate action.

Example: one problem, several symptoms

Three alerts describe checkout errors, payment timeouts and queue growth. Open one incident, choose severity, assign responsibility and link the three alerts. During investigation, you discover the queue alert belongs to a different ongoing problem: move it to that active incident. Record the diagnosis and mitigation in notes. After recovery, verify the remaining alerts and resolve the incident explicitly. If the problem returns, inspect current ownership before reopening or starting a new investigation.

Presets and dashboards

Guide contents · Previous: Incidents · Next: Maintenance and correlation

Choose a reusable view

Use a preset when you want a repeatable list of alerts, with useful columns and ordering. Use a dashboard when you want to monitor several preset counts together and open the underlying lists as needed. Both are shared with the tenant. There are no private saved presets or dashboards.

View full-size illustration

Temporary choices affect your current view. Saving a preset changes the definition used by everyone and by every dashboard panel that references it.

For example, a team could save a preset for its active payment alerts, another for critical alerts and another for recent recoveries. A dashboard can show their counts side by side. These groups can overlap: adding their counts together does not necessarily give the number of distinct alerts across all three.

Open and explore a preset

  1. Open Presets and search by name if necessary.
  2. Click the preset's row. Check its filter summary, which shows the saved expression, any saved grouping and the sort, and its columns.
  3. Use Search text, a quick view or Include suppressed for the immediate investigation. Press the selected quick view again to return to All.
  4. Click a supported sortable column heading to sort ascending; click again for descending; click a third time to restore the saved order.
  5. To see the results grouped another way, choose a field in Group by. The menu tags the preset's saved field Saved. Select Reset to return to the saved grouping.
  6. Open an alert row. Use Back to preset to return to its results.

Temporary text search, quick views, suppression inclusion, column-heading sorting and a Group by change in the results toolbar do not edit the shared preset. Reopening a preset starts from its saved definition, with text search empty, suppressed alerts excluded and the saved grouping. A new saved revision also clears a temporary sort or grouping. Grouped results work as described in Feed and search. Status is a derived display field and does not offer ordinary Status sorting; Lifecycle sorts the underlying firing/resolved state when selected in an editor.

The result count covers all matching logical alerts, not just the loaded page. The query true includes retained resolved alerts as well as firing alerts, subject to suppression exclusion. A preset is not automatically an active-alert view: read its actual filter.

Create a preset with Edit access

Use this when a search is useful to the whole tenant, for example as a standard shift view.

  1. Open Presets → New preset, or start from the Feed's Save as preset action.
  2. Enter a clear unique Name, such as Payments active alerts.
  3. Enter Filter (CEL). For this example, use application == "payments" && status == "firing", replacing payments with the actual application value. See Feed and search for the difference between raw lifecycle and visible status.
  4. Use Validate CEL for expression diagnostics and Preview draft to inspect matching current alerts. Ctrl+Enter also previews the expression.
  5. Under Columns, add the fields readers need and use the up/down controls to order them. Keep at least one column; up to 30 distinct supported columns are allowed.
  6. Under Sort, select one to three supported fields and their directions. A sort field need not be displayed as a column.
  7. Optionally, under Group by, choose a field so the results show one table per value, such as Application. Keep No grouping for a single table. Rows inside each group follow the sort above.
  8. Select Create preset. Reopen it and check that the saved filter, columns, order and grouping express the intended shared view.

The saved definition contains only the name, CEL expression, sort, columns and optional grouping. It does not save quick views, Search text, Include suppressed, a Group by change made in the results toolbar, collapsed groups, a page position or a separate time range. Put a condition into CEL if it must be part of the shared query. There is no separate time picker; supported time conditions also belong in CEL.

Previewing reads current data; it does not save, change alerts, trigger a rule or simulate historical states. A valid query that matches nothing can still be saved. If you change the expression after previewing, use Preview draft again before interpreting the displayed results as matches for the new text.

Change a shared preset carefully

  1. Open the preset. Before editing its query, inspect Dashboard references from the preset's options to understand which shared panels will be affected.
  2. Select Edit preset.
  3. Change the name, expression, columns, sorting or grouping and inspect the draft preview.
  4. Select Save changes to update the existing shared preset, or Save as new… and supply a unique name to create a second shared preset.

Changing the saved query changes every referencing dashboard's future counts. Changing only columns, sorting or grouping does not change which alerts those panels count. To change a preset's saved grouping, edit the preset; the Group by menu on its results changes only your current view. A rename keeps existing links because the preset's identity stays the same.

Use Discard to abandon a preset definition draft. If someone else saves first, Appchi retains your draft and reports a conflict. Review the newer definition before deciding what to keep. Save as new… creates another shared view; it is not a personal copy or an overwrite shortcut.

History shows retained changes. Where Use this revision is available, it loads an old definition into a draft; an explicit save creates a new revision after current validation. It does not erase intervening history. Deleted presets have retained history but no undelete action.

Deletion affects everyone. A preset that a current dashboard uses cannot be deleted: inspect Dashboard references, then have an editor remove or replace those panel references first. Deleting a preset never deletes its alerts.

Read a dashboard

  1. Open Boards, then choose a dashboard.
  2. Read each panel's title, exact count and any stale or unavailable marker.
  3. Select a panel to open the referenced preset's current results, grouped if the preset saves a grouping.
  4. Use Refresh counts if you need an explicit refresh, or Fullscreen for monitoring. Exit fullscreen to return to normal navigation and editing controls.

Each panel counts logical alert records matching its preset's current saved query. It does not count incoming messages, occurrences, incident links or notes. Dashboard counts exclude suppressed alerts and include resolved alerts unless the query excludes them. Dashboards have no Include suppressed switch; use the Feed or preset results to inspect those alerts.

A panel opens a fresh result view. New arrivals, changed alerts, a time-dependent query or a saved preset edit can make its result count differ from the number you just saw. Browser Back returns to the dashboard and refreshes it; it does not replay an earlier snapshot.

Zero means a successful query matched no alerts. Unavailable means the count could not be obtained. A stale number is a previous result and should not be treated as current. Automatic refresh keeps the last useful display while working; a failed query is never evidence of zero problems.

Create or edit a dashboard with Edit access

  1. Make sure the saved presets you need already exist in the same tenant.
  2. Open Boards → New dashboard, or open an existing dashboard.
  3. Click the dashboard name or its pen icon to rename it.
  4. Select Create panel. Choose a Saved preset and, optionally, a Custom title. Leaving the title blank uses the preset's name.
  5. Choose Width, Height and any Count thresholds, then select Create panel in the dialog. This changes only your draft.
  6. Repeat for the required panels, up to 24. Use each panel's options for Edit panel or Delete panel. To edit a panel quickly, point at it and press E.
  7. Arrange panels using the move grip and resize handle; arrow keys also work on either handle. Panels settle upward to close empty gaps. On narrow screens the dashboard shows the saved layout and panels cannot be rearranged.
  8. Select Save to save the entire shared name, panel configuration and layout together.

There is no separate dashboard editor. Apply changes in a panel dialog updates the draft; it does not replace the dashboard-level Save. A new or changed preset reference says Save the dashboard to count this panel until saved. Canceling a panel dialog leaves the existing draft unchanged.

The Unsaved changes chip identifies a changed draft. Save is disabled when there is nothing new to save. Discard changes asks for confirmation, then restores the current saved definition. Fullscreen hides editing controls, and automatic updates continue; there is no Pause updates control.

Use color thresholds as a visual aid

Thresholds compare the count to lower bounds. For example, set 1 to amber and 10 to red: zero remains neutral, 1–9 is amber and 10 or more is red. Up to three distinct increasing nonnegative whole-number bounds are supported. The largest bound reached determines the color.

Each threshold has a color button. Colors offers eight hues in five shades, from lightest to darkest; these follow the light or dark theme. Custom lets you choose any color with the color area and hue slider, or type a hex value such as #E0569B; a custom color looks the same in both themes. The preview in the panel dialog shows your choice at once, and the dashboard keeps it when you save.

A red panel is not necessarily a critical alert. Threshold colors are presentation only: they do not change severity, create incidents, notify anyone or take action. Read the preset and threshold meaning before drawing operational conclusions.

Share, reuse and review

You can share a normal Appchi link with a colleague who already has access to the tenant; the link does not grant permission or expose a public dashboard. Dashboard options provide History, Save as new… and eligible deletion. Copies keep references to the same saved presets, so later query changes affect both dashboards.

Deleting a dashboard removes that shared view and its current panel references, not the presets or alerts. Deleted history is read-only; there is no undelete. Retained configuration history has access limits, so it is not a permanent archive.

Maintenance and correlation

Guide contents · Previous: Presets and dashboards · Next: Troubleshooting and glossary

Maintenance explains suppression; correlation explains automatic incident grouping. Both are shared within the selected tenant.

Readers can inspect and test definitions. Editing requires edit access and responsibility for the shared definition.

Maintenance: keep expected activity in context

A maintenance window is a one-time interval with a condition describing the alerts it covers. Use it for planned work likely to produce expected alerts. Suppressed alerts continue to arrive, retain their history and remain accessible through Include suppressed in the Feed. Ordinary Feed results, counts and automatic correlation exclude them by default.

Suppression is separate from lifecycle: an alert may be firing and suppressed, or resolved and suppressed. Existing incident links remain intact. An alert can have multiple maintenance causes plus a separate source-suspension cause; it stays suppressed while any cause remains open.

View full-size illustration

Concept illustration. The end of the window stops new coverage. It does not immediately clear existing suppression.

Two rules that prevent surprises

No backfill. Creating, starting or enabling a window does not scan already-matching alerts and suppress them. Coverage needs a qualifying new alert or transition. A firing alert that matched before the window can remain unsuppressed; an unchanged repeat does not manufacture a new entry transition.

No automatic timer release. After a window ends, or is disabled or deleted, an already-suppressed alert can remain suppressed until its next source update received at or after that boundary. An unchanged source repeat can clear this ended cause. Refreshing the page or adding a note does not clear it. Another active cause may still keep the alert suppressed. Ending maintenance never resolves the alert.

For example, a qualifying alert becomes suppressed during a 10:00–11:00 window. At 11:05 the window reads Ended, but no later source input has arrived, so suppression remains. A source update received at 11:10 can clear that window's cause. If the source still suspends the alert independently, it remains suppressed.

Inspect a window

Open Maintenance, select the window, and check its reason, Timezone, Starts, Ends and Which alerts (CEL) condition. Distinguish its phase from its Enabled/Disabled setting. Ended describes the schedule; it does not prove that every alert cause has closed.

Review the alerts suppressed by this window and History when an alert's treatment is unexpected. A Blocked processing state needs investigation; do not assume it means “no suppression.” Test a current alert checks a selected alert without changing it. A current match is not proof that the required entry transition happened.

Create or adjust a window when authorized

  1. From Maintenance, select New window. Enter a name and reason for the planned work.
  2. Select the timezone and local start/end times. Use Validate schedule and review the converted times. If a daylight-saving transition repeats a local time, select the first or second occurrence; a nonexistent local time must be corrected.
  3. Enter the CEL expression describing the intended alerts. CEL is the condition language also used by the Feed. Use the field catalog and validation, then test a representative current alert.
  4. Select Create window. New windows start enabled. Remember that creation does not backfill existing matches.

Use Edit and Save maintenance change for an existing definition. Once its start has passed, the start time and condition are locked, even if the window was disabled. Before expiry, its end may be adjusted but must remain in the future. An ended window cannot be extended or re-enabled. Changing only the display timezone preserves the actual execution times.

Use Disable to stop new coverage without deleting the definition. Delete also stops new coverage and requires confirmation; neither action immediately releases existing causes. Recurring schedules are not available.

Correlation: why Appchi opened an incident

A correlation rule looks for fresh evidence that named conditions have been met within a shared time window, optionally grouped by fields such as an application. All conditions need evidence before the rule creates an incident. One alert can satisfy multiple conditions. Once the incident is active, another eligible alert matching any condition can be added.

Eligible alerts must be firing, unsuppressed and available for ownership. An assigned alert displayed as Acknowledged can still qualify. An alert already owned by another active incident cannot be taken automatically. When rules compete, earlier-created qualifying rules have precedence; users do not set a priority number.

For example, a rule requires checkout errors and payment timeouts within ten minutes, grouped by application. Fresh evidence for both conditions in the same group can open one incident. Other applications form separate groups. Later eligible evidence matching either condition can add alerts.

A match is not the same as fresh evidence

Correlation does not repeatedly search all current alerts or replay their full history. Creating or enabling a rule does not backfill old matches. Unchanged repeats do not refresh evidence or restore evidence that expired. Manually changing an alert does not create fresh source evidence.

After resolving an incident or unlinking an alert, old evidence cannot immediately recreate the same automatic ownership. Later qualifying source transitions are required. Suppression, a rule being disabled or deleted, or a condition ceasing to match does not remove existing incident links. Review those links as part of the investigation.

Inspect or test a rule

Follow an incident's Created by correlation rule link, or open Correlation rules. Review its conditions, grouping, Window (minutes), enabled setting and health. Current evidence explains what is currently supporting it; associated incidents show its results. History helps explain a change or reset.

Use the rule's test tool with a current tenant alert. It reports condition matches, grouping and exclusions without creating evidence, incidents or links. A successful test of one alert does not prove all conditions occurred within a real window. If the rule is blocked or delayed, report its name, health and visible diagnostic to the responsible colleague.

Edit shared rules with care

Authorized editors can select New rule, enter a name, explicitly choose incident severity, define 1–10 named conditions, set a window of 1–1,440 whole minutes and optionally select up to three grouping fields. Use Validate, test a representative alert, then Create rule. The rule starts enabled. Each generated incident starts open and unassigned.

Changing condition logic or the time window resets current evidence. Changing grouping also stops older-group incidents receiving automatic additions under the new grouping. Existing incidents retain their content and links. Disabling stops automatic creation/addition; re-enabling starts fresh evidence collection without backfill. Use these controls for an intentional shared behavior change, not as a way to force a missing incident into existence. For a current investigation, use the manual incident workflow in Working with incidents.

A dashboard count differs from an open list Check the preset, text/quick filters, suppression switch and freshness. A dashboard uses the saved query with suppression excluded; a list can have temporary filters and a newer snapshot.

Recover from access and save problems

What you see What to do
Assign, Save or Create is missing Check your tenant and role. View access permits investigation but not operational changes. Ask your tenant administrator if your duties require Edit access.
An assignee is missing from the picker Use an eligible person with access to the tenant. Ask an administrator about access or identity updates; typing a name cannot grant access.
A tenant or shared link is unavailable Choose an accessible tenant and check the link with its sender. Access may have changed, or the resource may have been deleted.
Session expired Sign in again. A background tab does not keep the session alive, and continuous activity does not remove the absolute session limit.
CEL validation failed Read the marked field or expression diagnostic. Use the editor's supported field suggestions, correct the expression and rerun it. An invalid query does not mean no matches.
Someone changed the record before you saved Reload/review the latest record and reconsider your action. For shared definitions, preserve your intended changes while comparing, or use Save as new if a separate shared view is appropriate.
A command timed out and the outcome is uncertain Use Retry original command or the specific retry offered by the page. Do not repeatedly create a new record to guess whether the first request worked. Inspect the current state when it becomes available.
Preset deletion is blocked Open Dashboard references. An editor must remove or replace active references before deleting that preset.
Dashboard Save is disabled There may be no changes, a panel dialog may still be open, or another command may be in progress. Apply or cancel the dialog and check the feedback.
New panel has no count Save the dashboard after choosing its saved preset. The panel dialog alone does not commit it.

Live updates degraded · reconnecting, Updates unavailable or Catching up · counts may lag beside the page actions means the display may be catching up; use Refresh where it is offered. Keep the distinction between the latest loaded detail and the search result list. If the condition persists or affects a decision, use your organization's support route.

Avoid common workflow mistakes

  • Assignment records responsibility; it does not repair a service or send a notification.
  • Resolving an incident does not resolve its linked alerts, and resolved alerts do not automatically resolve their incident.
  • Suppression keeps alerts stored. It is neither deletion nor resolution.
  • A new maintenance window does not sweep all existing matching alerts into suppression.
  • A rule test or query preview does not perform the operation it describes.
  • Shared Save changes what other tenant users see. Save as new is also shared.
  • A source can update an alert again after your manual action. Read the new occurrence and activity rather than assuming the earlier command failed.
  • There is no automatic inactivity resolution or retention sweep in the current release. An incident stays active until someone resolves it, so close neglected work explicitly.

Give support a useful report

Include the tenant, page or record link, what you expected, what happened, and the approximate time with timezone. Include the exact visible error or request identifier if provided, the relevant filter, and whether the problem affects one record or many. For a save problem, say whether the page reported failure, conflict or an uncertain outcome.

Use your organization's approved channel. Keep passwords, API keys and session information out of the report. Review screenshots and alert contents before sharing beyond the people authorized to read them. Your tenant administrator handles access and shared configuration questions; the Appchi support team handles persistent application failures.

Quick reference for handoff

Before leaving an investigation, check the correct tenant, the current alert and incident owners, a note stating findings and next action, unresolved linked alerts, and any suppression that could hide continuing work. Confirm a shared definition was saved if you meant others to use it. Tell the next person through your normal team channel; Appchi does not notify them automatically.
